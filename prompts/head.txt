EXPRESSION CONSISTENCY — FRONT VIEW IS THE ANCHOR:
Treat the sheet as the SAME head at the SAME instant, with only the viewing angle changing. Establish the front-view expression from the approved whole-character front reference. When editing an existing head sheet, preserve its front-view expression as the anchor unless the user explicitly requests changing it. Derive both profiles from that exact expression; do not independently sample a new expression for each view or average inconsistent side-view expressions from the references.
Keep mouth opening, lip separation, jaw opening/position, smile tension, exposed upper/lower teeth, and any visible tongue consistent in 3D across front, left and right views. If the front mouth is open with separated jaws, both profiles must show the corresponding jaw opening and lip gap; do not switch to closed lips or clenched teeth. If the front mouth is closed, do not open it in a profile. Preserve eyebrow expression, eyelid openness and gaze relative to the head, allowing normal foreshortening and occlusion. Teeth/lips need not have identical 2D outlines or visible counts at different angles, but must represent the same expression and jaw configuration. Preserve intended facial asymmetry on the correct anatomical side; do not mirror markings or expressions. The back view shows only anatomy actually visible from behind, with no invented facial features.
Before returning the sheet, compare both profiles against the front for open-versus-closed mouth, jaw position, teeth visibility and brow/eye expression. Correct expression mismatches while preserving identity, neck boundaries and layout.
